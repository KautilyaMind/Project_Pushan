# Rice Irrigation and Safe Alternate Wetting and Drying

> **Prototype knowledge document for demonstration purposes.** It summarizes educational principles and is not a site-specific rice prescription. Safe use depends on production system, soil, groundwater, field levelling, water control, cultivar, weeds, and local guidance.

## Water control first

Rice water management requires the ability to add and remove water predictably. Maintain bunds, inlets, outlets, and drainage; observe field variation rather than measuring only an unusually high or low spot. Rainfall and upstream delivery can change conditions quickly.

## What alternate wetting and drying means

Alternate wetting and drying (AWD) is an IRRI-developed water-management practice for irrigated lowland rice in which ponded and non-ponded conditions alternate. It is managed by measured subsurface water level, not by waiting for severe crop stress or cracked soil. Suitability must be assessed locally; uncontrolled rainfall or unreliable water delivery can make precise implementation difficult.

## Field water tube

IRRI describes a perforated field water tube that allows the below-surface water level to be seen. A representative installation should exchange water freely with the surrounding soil. If the level inside does not equilibrate with the field after several hours, holes may be blocked or installation may be faulty. Keep the measurement point accessible and away from unusual high or low positions.

## Safe AWD reference procedure

IRRI's field guidance uses approximately 15 cm below the soil surface as the re-irrigation reference in safe AWD and refloods to roughly 5 cm. The precise implementation requires local confirmation. The period around flowering is protected: IRRI advises maintaining flooded conditions from about one week before through one week after flowering, topping up as needed. AWD is normally introduced only after establishment; weed pressure may justify delaying it.

Do not transfer the 15 cm field-water-level reference to a volumetric soil-moisture sensor as though the numbers were interchangeable. They are different measurements. A sensor-based implementation must be calibrated against field water level, soil, and crop response.

## Operational checklist

1. Confirm the field is suitable and water delivery/drainage can be controlled.
2. Verify crop establishment, stage, and weed-management implications.
3. Inspect bunds and locate representative field-water tubes.
4. Confirm tube openings are clear and readings equilibrate.
5. Record ponded depth, subsurface level, rainfall, and irrigation events.
6. Protect the flowering window from water deficit according to validated local practice.
7. Avoid excessive reflooding, uncontrolled spill, and unmeasured delivery.
8. Stop or modify the schedule when heavy rainfall, drainage limitations, salinity, or local agronomic advice makes AWD inappropriate.

## Interpreting Pushan answers

The RAG assistant may explain safe AWD from these sources. It must not command a pump or assume every rice field is eligible. Operational feasibility, sensor validity, and deterministic safety rules remain outside RAG.
