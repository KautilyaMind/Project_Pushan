# Cotton Irrigation: Growth-Stage and Root-Zone Guidance

> **Prototype knowledge document for demonstration purposes.** It is educational and not a universal schedule. Use locally validated cotton, soil, climate, variety, and irrigation-system recommendations.

## Stage-aware management

Cotton does not have one constant water requirement. FAO training material identifies flowering and boll formation as particularly sensitive to shortage. Establishment, vegetative growth, yield formation, and maturation should therefore be distinguished in records and scheduling.

## Establishment and vegetative growth

During establishment, verify that water reaches the emerging or transplanted root zone and that crusting, ponding, or runoff is not undermining stand development. As the root system and canopy expand, revise the effective rooting depth and stored-water estimate. Avoid making a full-season schedule from an early-season root depth.

## Flowering and boll formation

Give this period close attention because avoidable water stress can affect reproductive development. Use calibrated root-zone measurements and/or an ET water balance. Plan early enough for pump and system capacity: waiting until visible severe stress may be too late. “Closer attention” does not mean uncontrolled irrigation; applications should still be constrained by the measured root-zone deficit and infiltration capacity.

## Late season

Late-season management must consider crop maturity, remaining boll development, soil storage, weather, and local harvest strategy. Do not extend a peak-demand schedule automatically through maturation. Likewise, do not terminate water solely because a calendar date has arrived.

## Scheduling evidence to combine

- Crop stage and locally appropriate root depth.
- Calibrated soil-water measurements at representative positions and depths.
- ETc or a local crop-water-use estimate.
- Effective rainfall and measured irrigation.
- Soil available-water capacity and infiltration rate.
- Forecast demand, irrigation-system capacity, uniformity, and downtime risk.
- Signs of runoff, deep drainage, salinity, waterlogging, or equipment failure.

## Sensor interpretation

A volumetric soil-moisture number has no universal cotton meaning. Relate it to the specific soil's field capacity, allowable depletion, sensor calibration, depth, and root activity. Multiple locations help expose spatial variability. Investigate an implausibly constant value or disagreement with field condition before using it operationally.

## Practical response to shortage or limited capacity

When supply is constrained, prioritize using validated stage sensitivity, actual field deficit, system capacity, and local professional advice. Record any deficit strategy explicitly. RAG can summarize the relevant documents; it cannot safely allocate water among fields or override deterministic operational controls.
